1. Summary & decision
The HIM is a small heated IMU daughtercard that mounts onto the AeroNode carrier and exposes its sensors to the host STM32H7. We have dropped the ADIS16470 (large BGA, costly) in favour of a triple consumer-MEMS stack matching the current Pixhawk 6X / CUAV X7 family, with the heater plus ArduPilot temperature calibration recovering the bias stability the ADIS would have given. No BGA — straightforward assembly on Black Box’s pick-and-place line, in an ~18–22 mm-square module.
Design principle: the whole module is the thermally-isolated, soft-mounted mass. Rather than an internal slot-moat, the small module itself is the isolated island — heater under the cluster, foam cap on top, silicone/gel soft-mounts to the carrier. The host H7 owns all SPI; the module is pure sensor + heat.

3. Heater & thermal design
Element: 4× 2512 (6332 metric) power resistors ≈ 5 Ω total, on the backside directly under the IMU cluster, spread so the heat centroid sits at the cluster centroid.
Coupling: 2 oz copper spreader pour on top, inner and backside layers, tied with a dense thermal-via array (~0.3 mm vias on ~1 mm grid) — turns the FR4 sandwich into a uniform hot-plate so all three IMUs see the same temperature.
Drive: logic-level low-side N-FET (AO3400-class) on the module, gate from host H7 timer PWM (100 Ω series, 100 kΩ pulldown). Place the FET away from the cluster.
Feedback: IMU internal temperature registers (primary); 10 kΩ NTC central to the cluster → host ADC as an independent over-temp watchdog (~80 °C trip).
Setpoint: 45 °C flight; ~60–70 °C transiently during temp-cal. Sizing: ~5–6 W peak warm-up, ~2–3 W hold (see IMU Front-End spec, Appendix A).
Mechanical: foam cap over the IMUs to trap heat and block convective currents; soft-mount the module to the carrier.
4. Interface to the carrier
Single board-to-board connector (e.g. Hirose DF40C-20DP-0.4V, 20-pin). Route the IMUs as 1–2 SPI buses with separate chip-selects; expose the bus(es) plus interrupts and the heater/NTC lines:

5. Power
IMU rail: low-noise 3.3 V LDO (ADP151 / TPS7A20-class) on the module; per-IMU decoupling and a ferrite to isolate supply noise. A single quiet LDO is acceptable at this size; use two if area allows.
Heater rail: 5 V passed through from the carrier, switched by the on-module FET.

7. Firmware & calibration
hwdef: define the SPI bus(es), three IMU drivers (Invensensev3 ×2, BMI088), CS + INT pins, plus HAL_HAVE_IMU_HEATER on the PWM pin and the IMU temperature source.
Params: BRD_HEAT_TARG=45, heater PID gains, EK3_IMU_MASK to enable all three lanes.
Production temp-cal (important): with no ADIS factory calibration, run ArduPilot’s IMU temperature calibration on every unit — a per-module temperature sweep that captures bias/scale coefficients. Plan a simple oven/chamber fixture for this in production.
8. Build & test notes
Bench: confirm each IMU enumerates and INT toggles; verify heater PID reaches and holds 45 °C; log warm-up time and current.
Thermal uniformity: thermal-image or multi-point probe to confirm all three IMUs sit within a few °C; tune resistor spread / add a backside perimeter resistor only if edges run cool.
Bias stability: Allan-variance before vs after heater + temp-cal; compare the three lanes.
Vibration: soft-mount tuning + harmonic-notch setup on the airframe (helicopter needs iteration).
9. Mechanical / stackup
4-layer PCB, ~18–22 mm square; copper pour on inner layers for the thermal spreader.
Module = the isolated mass: soft-mount to carrier via silicone/gel; foam cap over the IMU cluster.
Keep the heater FET and the connector away from the IMU cluster zone.
This is a development-stage module for R&D, not a certified product. See the IMU Front-End and DVNC specifications for the wider AeroNode context and safety framing.

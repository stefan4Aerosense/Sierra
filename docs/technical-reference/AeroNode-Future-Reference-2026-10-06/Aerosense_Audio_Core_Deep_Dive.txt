One idea shapes everything: the AeroNode — a crash-protected 
processing + power hub, hard-mounted to the airframe — decouples 
heavy computation from the headset. That is what lets the audio stack 
do things a standalone headset cannot (a clean airframe vibration 
reference for DVNC; a high-rate attitude source for VGSA/DSMA). 
The H1 earcup runs only the hard-real-time inner loops; the 
AeroNode does the smart, non-real-time work.

1.2 The seams every audio subsystem shares 
•One earcup driver + one in-cup error mic — Noise Cancelling and DVNC both drive them; their two anti-

single priority/mixing scheme must arbitrate them (§3.6).

3. Subsystem deep dives (the working blocks) 
3.1 Noise Cancelling — acoustic hybrid ANC 
What & why. The conventional acoustic (mic-reference) ANC path for the H1 
earcup — feedforward (exterior) + feedback (in-cup) mics driving an in-cup 
canceller. It is the certifiable, self-contained baseline that works with the headset alone, 
and the ANC pillar for the December H1 Flight Demo. GA cockpit noise is 90–110 
dB, LF-dominated; prop fundamental f0 = RPM × blade count ≈ 70–300 Hz. ANC 
owns 20–~1000 Hz; passive handles ≥1 kHz. Continuous LF processing is 
“acoustic fatigue” — cutting it is the human-performance payoff. 
How. Committed architecture is hybrid AI-DSP: a real-time FxLMS (Filtered-x LMS) 
loop generates anti-noise (needs an accurate factory-identified secondary path 
Ŝ(z), driver→error-mic); a lightweight CRN (Convolutional Recurrent Network) 
supervises — it classifies the acoustic scene (“Cessna prop”, “helicopter rotor”, 
“jet wind”) and retunes FxLMS step-size/coefficients, without sitting in the sample 
loop. Topology per the 10 Jul steer: hybrid feedforward+feedback + DVNC as a 
third reference. A fail-safe analog passthrough (comms works with the DSP powered 
down) is a non-negotiable V3 criterion.

3.2 DVNC™ — vibration-referenced ANC 
What & why. A vibration-referenced ANC layer (not a replacement) that uses the 
airframe-mounted AeroNode as a clean, predictive reference. The AeroNode 
extracts the engine/prop fundamental + harmonics through structure (IMU) and 
air (mic) and streams parameters (ω0, phase, per-harmonic amplitude — kilobits, 
not audio) to the H1, which synthesises matched anti-noise for the ~20–250 Hz 
tonal rumble. Defensibility is structural: no standalone headset (Bose A30, 
Lightspeed Delta Zulu) has an airframe-mounted reference node. 
How — and the honest framing. H1 synthesises x(n) = [sin ω0n, cos ω0n, sin 2ω0n, 
…]; secondary path Ŝ(z) factory-fixed FIR; y(n)=Wᵀ(n)x(n); W(n+1)=W(n) − µ·e(n)·x′
(n), driven by the earcup error mic. Because vibration travels the airframe 
(~3,000–5,000 m/s) far faster than sound in air (~343 m/s), the AeroNode knows 
the engine state before the sound arrives. Do not overclaim: for a steady tone a 
phase-locked reference already cancels well — the defensible wins are (a) a 
clutter-free reference (deeper, faster convergence) and (b) tracking through RPM 
transients. P(z) (airframe→ear) is not static — needs continuous adaptation + dual-
IMU head tracking.

3.3 PureVoice™ — voice capture & enhancement 
What & why. The pilot-voice capture + enhancement chain (into comms, AeroVault 
recording, Creator Mode). The governing decision is already made: Path B — 
discriminative enhancement — is the committed primary path; generative resynthesis 
is retained only as an extreme-SNR fallback. Principle: “preserve and attenuate; 
never fabricate.” Worst failure is over-suppression (muffled/dropped consonant) — 
recoverable, and never a substitution. It fails toward noisy-but-correct, not clean-but-
wrong. Generative resynthesis can confidently synthesise a wrong word 
(“descend” for “ascend”, a corrupted altitude digit) — so it is out of the safety 
path. Certifiability was the deciding factor. 
How. Three decoupled problems. Capture: multi-sensor fusion — NC boom mic 
(air, primary) + in-earcup reference mic (doubles as ANC ref) + bone-conduction 
sensor (temple/concha piezo). Clean: 2-mic adaptive Griffiths-Jim cancellation → 
compact mask-based neural denoiser (bounded gain). Encode: low-delay codec 
(Opus-LD / LC3plus). Two latency budgets: the sidetone loop (own-voice monitor) is 
hard — DAF disruption climbs ~50→200 ms, so the monitor never includes the 
codec/radio; the transmit path (~20–30 ms) carries the heavy enhancement. 
When — status vs gate 
WS-D differentiator · OFF the demo critical path · reference engine demonstrable by V2, matures Months 
6–12. Concept validated (AeroNode IMU+mic engine signature captured on the airframe). Next: offline Python 
proof on the recorded signatures. Bench work waits on dev-kit delivery. IP: DVNC is the filing focus but not yet 
filed — keep disclosure high-level.
On the day — to align / decide 
•Link: wired CAN/RS-485 vs UWB/proprietary RF — determinism + latency + clock/phase-sync budget 
(the requirement that makes DVNC work). 
•Accelerometer selection/placement + mounting/vibration isolation for a clean structural reference (joint 
with AeroNode). 
•P(z) strategy: pre-trained per airframe vs continuous online; validity across aircraft types. 
•NC↔DVNC summation at the shared earcup: one engine vs parallel-and-sum.

3.4 VGSA™ — spatial-audio virtual horizon 
What & why. Experimental, emergency-only assistance for spatial disorientation in 
IMC. The AeroNode's high-rate IMU drives a 3D binaural “virtual horizon” that 
wraps the pilot's head and shifts with pitch/roll/yaw, prompting instinctive 
corrective inputs — a non-visual auditory horizon that persists when the visual 
one is gone. Spatial disorientation holds ~15% of fatal mishaps. 
How. Localisation from ITD (<1500 Hz, azimuth), IID (>1500 Hz, azimuth) and 
spectral/pinna filtering (>4 kHz, elevation); binaural cues alone can't resolve 
elevation or front/back, so the HRTF does. HRTFs update in real time with head 
tracking so the source stays locked to the aircraft frame. Attitude from the 
AeroNode IMU; head orientation from the H1's own IMU (relative to aircraft); real-
time HRTF convolution → binaural on the H1 drivers. Latency is safety-critical — the 
corrective response breaks if head-motion-to-audio lag is too high (hence the 
wired link, <1 ms). 
When — status vs gate 
WS-D (D-Voice) · off the demo critical path · gate RV-1. Committed to Path B. RV-1 evidence work = the M3–
4 neural-vocoder bench investigation (due ~1 Oct). Mic-array geometry freeze rising in urgency now that headset 
ID CAD has started.

owns which mics — freeze before either side commits.

3.5 DSMA™ — audio delivery of stall-margin cues 
What & why (audio scope). DSMA estimates synthetic airspeed (EKF on 
IMU+GPS+baro), computes the dynamic stall speed from G-load [V_stall(dyn) = 
V_stall(base) × √n], and delivers a safety-margin “headroom” cue through the 
headset — not a binary stall horn. States: SAFE (silent) → CAUTION (soft 
“Airspeed decaying”) → EMERGENCY (priority “WARNING: STALL MARGIN 
CRITICAL”). For John the relevant part is the audio delivery: how the headroom is 
encoded to sound, injected into the pilot's stream, and prioritised against 
everything else. 
How (audio path). In the PoC bench a dedicated audio DSP (ADAU1701-class) 
mixes TTS warning triggers (sent from the Pi over analog line-in) into the ANC 
audio stream; flap state uses a Picovoice Porcupine wake-word engine on the 
intercom (offloaded to a Coral Edge TPU so audio has no latency). In production 
this becomes the H1 audio path + the audio-arbitration contract.

This one interface enables DVNC and VGSA and gates the fail-safe 
story, so it deserves its own block. It must carry DVNC parameters 
(kilobits — ω0/phase/harmonics) and VGSA/DSMA/PilotSense 
audio with deterministic, low-latency, phase-coherent timing. Standard 
Bluetooth (20–100 ms, variable) is disqualified for the real-time 
path; BT 5.x stays for comms/media/AeroSync. Candidates: wired 
CAN/RS-485-class (front-runner in the DEV view — clock/phase sync 
is the real requirement, not bandwidth) or UWB/proprietary RF. The 
same cable/connector carries the fail-safe analog passthrough — 
which is why the switch-location decision (H1 vs AeroNode) is shared 
between Noise Cancelling and this link.

8. Safety & IP notes 
•Safety framing (mandatory). VGSA, DSMA and the PilotSense alerts are experimental, advisory, 
emergency-only aids — they augment pilot awareness and do not replace certified avionics or training. The 
AeroNode has zero input to flight controls. Noise Cancelling/DVNC are comfort features; the fail-safe analog 
passthrough guarantees comms with the DSP powered down, and the H1 always works as a passive aviation 
audio headset. 
•FTO. Risk concentrates in the in-cup acoustic canceller (Noise Cancelling, Zone 3). The FxLMS/FxNLMS + 
open-source-model route is the independent-derivation defence. V on Seidels ANR landscape Phase 1 is 
underway; check any in-cup design choice against Zone 3 before committing silicon. 
•DVNC disclosure. DVNC is the differentiator and filing focus but is NOT yet among the filed provisionals 
— keep external disclosure high-level until filed. Treat this document as confidential IP under development.

1. Summary
A single high-quality MEMS microphone serves two roles on the AeroNode: it provides the DVNC acoustic reference (low-frequency engine/prop signature for the noise-cancelling layer) and the AeroVault™ cabin-audio stream (full-bandwidth forensic recording for the personal black box). This is a recording-chain configuration, not two microphones: capture the mic wideband (20 Hz–20 kHz, 48 kHz / 24-bit) for AeroVault, and derive the low-band DVNC reference by filtering/decimating the same stream. Full scope is captured; the low band is prioritised for cancellation.
2. Microphone selection
The cockpit is loud (90–110 dB SPL, louder transients) and low-frequency rich, so the priorities are a high acoustic overload point (AOP), a flat low-frequency response, and high SNR.

2.1 Why these specs
AOP 128–135 dB SPL: crash and alarm transients are extreme; high AOP keeps the loudest, most forensically valuable moments from clipping — exactly when the recording matters most.
Flat to 20 Hz: captures the full 20–250 Hz rumble band DVNC targets; cheaper mics roll off at 50–100 Hz and miss the lowest rumble.
High SNR (72–73 dB): clean DVNC reference and intelligible AeroVault voice/audio.
Tight phase/sensitivity matching: enables a multi-mic array for redundancy, zone coverage and beamforming.
3. Signal architecture — one mic, two streams
The single wideband capture feeds two consumers in parallel:
AeroVault (full scope): the 48 kHz / 24-bit wideband master — voice, alarms/horns, switch clicks, airflow, structural and impact sounds across the full audible band.
DVNC (low band): a filtered/decimated tap of the same stream for the 20–250 Hz acoustic reference — no second microphone required.
This keeps the forensic high-end and the cancellation low-end from competing: AeroVault keeps the wideband master; DVNC takes a filtered tap.
4. Coverage & redundancy (CVR-style)
Design for 2–4 matched IM72D128 mics: redundancy if one fails in an impact, plus zone coverage and future beamforming / voice-from-engine separation.
Populate one to start; lay out the matched footprints so additional mics are a populate-only change.

6. Interface & routing
Digital (IM72D128, PDM): route PDM to the STM32H7 SAI/DFSDM peripheral directly (no codec), or via a PDM-to-I²S step to the CM5 for the recording/storage path. AeroVault storage lives on the CM5/Pi.
Analog (IM73A135): through a low-noise audio ADC/codec to the host.
Time-sync: clock the mic from a common reference and timestamp samples so the acoustic and IMU (structure-borne) references align for DVNC and the AeroVault timeline.
7. Mounting & acoustics
Acoustic port: enclosure port with mesh/windscreen over the mic sound inlet; keep the port path short and sealed.
Vibration decoupling: mechanically isolate the mic from board/chassis vibration (gasket/soft mount) — the mic must capture cabin air, not chassis vibration (that is the IMU's role).
Placement: position to sample representative cabin air; for a multi-mic array, separate the mics for zone/directional coverage.

9. Optional — beyond audible
For mechanical-failure precursors (bearing/gearbox signatures often appear ultrasonically), the IM73A135-class parts extend above 20 kHz; sampling at 96 kHz captures this. A predictive-maintenance / forensics nice-to-have, not required for v1.
10. Limitations & safety framing
AeroVault™ is an experimental/advisory “personal black box,” modelled on cockpit-voice-recorder principles but NOT a certified CVR. It augments post-event understanding and does not replace certified flight recorders or avionics. The AeroNode is a portable electronic device with zero input to the aircraft or its flight controls. This document describes a development-stage subsystem, not a certified product.
